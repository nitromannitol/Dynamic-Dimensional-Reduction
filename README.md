# Dynamic-Dimensional-Reduction

A **Lean 4** formalization, in progress, of the paper
[*Dynamic dimensional reduction in the Abelian sandpile*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.05968)
(Ahmed Bou-Rabee, Communications in Mathematical Physics 390 (2022), 933-958;
arXiv:2009.05968), built on [`mathlib`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4) and
the shared library [`Lattice-Probability`](https://github.com/nitromannitol/Lattice-Probability)
(`LatticeProb`).

[![CI](https://github.com/nitromannitol/Dynamic-Dimensional-Reduction/actions/workflows/build.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/nitromannitol/Dynamic-Dimensional-Reduction/actions/workflows/build.yml)

## What this is

Start with `2d` chips at every site of the hypercube `C_N^{(d)} ⊆ ℤ^d` and topple in
parallel: at each step every site holding at least `2d` chips sends one chip to each
neighbour, and chips that leave the cube are lost.  The odometer `v_t^{(d,N)}(x)` counts the
topplings at `x` by time `t`.  The main theorem of the paper (Theorem 1.1) says that, on the
sorted fundamental domain `S_M^{(d)} = {M ≥ x_1 ≥ ⋯ ≥ x_d ≥ 1}` with `M = ⌈N/2⌉`,

    v_t^{(d,N)}(x_{d-1}, 1) = v_t^{(d-1,N)}(x_{d-1})      for every t ≥ 1,

so the centre slice of the `d`-dimensional sandpile runs the `(d-1)`-dimensional one for all
time (dimensional reduction), that the terminal odometers agree one layer further in, and
that the odometers of cubes of different sizes agree near their corners up to a topple time
`τ_j` (self-similarity).  Corollary 1.1 transfers both statements to the sandpile itself.
The proof goes through a discrete parabolic regularity of the odometer: Section 2 proves a
least action principle, symmetry and derivative comparisons, Section 3 computes the
odometer explicitly on cubes of side 2 and 4, and Sections 4 and 5 prove Proposition 4.1
(five regularity clauses) and Theorem 1.1 together, by one induction over dimension, side
length and time.

**This is a work in progress.**  Every theorem, lemma, proposition and corollary of the
paper is registered as a frozen Lean statement (`tools/check_coverage.py`), 20 registered
statements in all.  **10 are proved and 10 are open.**  The open ones are the three clauses
of Theorem 1.1, the two clauses of Corollary 1.1 and the five clauses of Proposition 4.1,
that is, the whole joint induction of Sections 4 and 5, so **none of the paper's main
results is proved yet**.  The proved ones are every result of Sections 2 and 3: Lemmas 2.1
to 2.5, Corollaries 2.1 and 2.2, Proposition 3.1, Lemma 3.1 and Proposition 3.2.  Not
formalized: the introduction's discussion, the open problems of Section 1 and the
figures.

**Nothing is assumed.**  No result the paper cites is taken as a hypothesis;
[`ASSUMPTIONS.md`](ASSUMPTIONS.md) records this.

- **`sorry`:** 10 registered statements are still open, each a single registered `sorry`;
  everything else is proved with only the three standard axioms.
  `tools/check_warnings.py` fails on any other `sorry` or warning.
- **No custom `axiom`.**  The proved results reduce to `mathlib`'s three standard
  foundational axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, verified by
  [`DimRed/Meta/AxiomsAudit.lean`](DimRed/Meta/AxiomsAudit.lean) for the results restated in
  [`DimRed/MainTheorems.lean`](DimRed/MainTheorems.lean) and by `tools/check_axioms.py` for
  every registered statement.
- Pinned to Lean `v4.32.0`, `mathlib` at revision
  `81a5d257c8e410db227a6665ed08f64fea08e997` and `Lattice-Probability` at commit
  `bbe0b90`.

## Main results

None of the paper's main results is proved yet.  [`DimRed/MainTheorems.lean`](DimRed/MainTheorems.lean)
names their frozen statements in its docstring (`DimRed.Frozen.dimensional_reduction`,
`DimRed.Frozen.terminal_dimensional_reduction` and `DimRed.Frozen.self_similarity` for
Theorem 1.1, `DimRed.Frozen.sandpile_dimensional_reduction` and
`DimRed.Frozen.sandpile_self_similarity` for Corollary 1.1), and states in full only the
results that are proved, each by `exact` of its certified counterpart in `DimRed/Frozen/`,
so the statements displayed there are the certified ones.  None takes a cited result as a
hypothesis.

* **`DimRed.pt_ind_lemma`** (Lemma 2.1): the parallel toppling recursion
  `v_{t+1}(x) = ⌊(s_0(x) + ∑_{y ∼ x} v_t(y)) / 2d⌋` for `0 ≤ s_0 ≤ 2(2d) - 1`.
* **`DimRed.parabolic_comparison`** (Lemma 2.2): the parabolic least action principle.
* **`DimRed.symmetry_lemma`** (Lemma 2.3): the odometer of a symmetric pile is invariant
  under the symmetries of the hypercube.
* **`DimRed.general_difference_comparison`** (Lemma 2.4): the derivative comparison.
* **`DimRed.axis_monotonicity`** (Corollary 2.1) and
  **`DimRed.derivative_bound`** (Corollary 2.2).
* **`DimRed.topple_limit`** (Lemma 2.5): weak topple control.
* **`DimRed.no_reduction_at_critical_dimension`** (Proposition 3.1): dimensional reduction
  fails on the cube of side 2.
* **`DimRed.radial_laplacian`** (Lemma 3.1) and **`DimRed.base_case`** (Proposition 3.2):
  the explicit odometer on the cube of side 4.

## Scope and faithfulness

Every theorem, lemma, proposition and corollary of the paper is registered as a Lean
declaration.  There are 20 registered statements: the 12 numbered results of Sections 1 to
3, with Theorem 1.1 split into its three displays and Corollary 1.1 into its two, and the
five clauses of Proposition 4.1.  The following words have fixed meanings.

| term | meaning |
|---|---|
| **registered statement**, also called a **node** | one Lean declaration transcribing one statement of the paper. Each has an entry in `ledger/manifest.yaml`. |
| **frozen statement** | the declaration's text between the lines `-- FROZEN-STATEMENT-BEGIN` and `-- FROZEN-STATEMENT-END`, pinned by the SHA-256 of those bytes. It cannot change without the change being recorded in the manifest. The proof after the end marker may be rewritten freely. |
| **`SEALED`** | the node is **proved** here: a complete proof, whose axiom closure contains only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. |
| **`DRAFT_SORRY`** | the node is **open**: its statement is frozen and its proof is a single registered `sorry`. |
| **axiom closure** | the axioms a proof ultimately rests on, as reported by Lean's `#print axioms`. |
| **`sorryAx`** | the axiom Lean inserts for an unproved `sorry`; its presence in an axiom closure means the result is not proved. |

Every registered statement is frozen in the sense of being pinned by hash, whether or not it
is proved yet.  The pins are what make the open statements meaningful: a later proof has to
prove the statement registered here, not a weaker one.

The paper is `paper/dimred.tex`, the arXiv source with the corrections listed in
[`paper/CHANGES_FROM_ARXIV.md`](paper/CHANGES_FROM_ARXIV.md), and every frozen statement
states what that file states.  [`CORRESPONDENCE.md`](CORRESPONDENCE.md) records the
vocabulary, the standing hypotheses the frozen statements write out, and the junk values.

Checkers compare the Lean statements with the paper's rather than with their hashes.
`tools/check_coverage.py` confirms that every theorem, lemma, proposition and corollary of
`paper/dimred.tex` is claimed by a node.  In each frozen statement whose conclusion has an
existential constant, `tools/check_constants.py` checks that it is bound before the paper's
parameters.  `tools/check_clauses.py` and `tools/check_exponents.py` compare assertion
counts and exponents; neither has a recorded reading of any node yet, so both currently
check nothing.  These are review aids; none proves equivalence between LaTeX and Lean.

## Building

The project uses [`elan`](https://github.com/leanprover/elan) (the Lean toolchain manager)
and Lake.  The toolchain is pinned in [`lean-toolchain`](lean-toolchain)
(`leanprover/lean4:v4.32.0`), so `elan` installs the right Lean version automatically, and
the dependencies are pinned to commits in `lakefile.lean` and
[`lake-manifest.json`](lake-manifest.json); `lake` fetches them, and there is nothing to
clone by hand.

```bash
lake exe cache get   # prebuilt Mathlib
lake build           # compile the project
```

The build emits exactly one `declaration uses 'sorry'` warning for each open registered
statement, 10 in all, and no other warning.

```bash
lake build DimRed.Meta.AxiomsAudit   # print the axioms of the proved results
```

To use the library, `import DimRed` pulls in the whole development; the proved results are
in `import DimRed.MainTheorems`.

The checkers in `tools/` need Python 3.10 or newer and PyYAML
(`python3 -m pip install -r tools/requirements.txt`).  `python3 tools/verify.py` runs every
check below and fails on the first failure; continuous integration runs it on request.
`check_axioms.py` and `check_warnings.py` run Lean; the others read the manifest, the paper
and the Lean sources.

| command | what it guarantees |
|---|---|
| `python3 tools/check_manifest.py` | every node's file holds exactly one frozen block whose SHA-256 equals `frozen_sha256` and which declares exactly the node's `export`; every file in `DimRed/Frozen/` belongs to one node; `DimRed/` contains no `axiom`, `admit` or `sorryAx`, and `sorry` only once in the proof of each open node |
| `python3 tools/check_axioms.py` | runs `#print axioms` on every manifest export and lists which closures contain `sorryAx`; it fails while any node is open, and `tools/verify.py` checks instead that exactly the open nodes are the ones that do |
| `python3 tools/check_warnings.py` | `lake build DimRed` emits one `sorry` warning per open node and no other warning |
| `python3 tools/check_constants.py` | in a frozen statement whose conclusion has an existential constant, the constant is bound before the paper's parameters |
| `python3 tools/check_coverage.py` | every theorem, lemma, proposition and corollary of `paper/dimred.tex` is named in the `source` of some manifest node |
| `python3 tools/check_clauses.py` | compares the assertion count of each paper statement with the conjunct count of its Lean statement, for the nodes with a recorded reading (none yet) |
| `python3 tools/check_exponents.py` | compares the exponents written in a paper statement with those in its Lean statement, for the nodes with a recorded reading (none yet) |

Also in `tools/`: `paper_anchors.py` and `paper_citations.py` check the `dimred.tex:<lines>`
ranges in the manifest and in the Lean docstrings against the paper's labels;
`sync_docs.py` checks (`--write` regenerates) the status block and the paper map of this
file and the frozen-surface table of `CORRESPONDENCE.md`; `assumptions.py` regenerates
`ASSUMPTIONS.md` (`--check` verifies it is current); `freeze.py` registers a frozen
statement in the manifest.

The paper `paper/dimred.tex` is the LaTeX source of arXiv:2009.05968v2 with the corrections
listed in [`paper/CHANGES_FROM_ARXIV.md`](paper/CHANGES_FROM_ARXIV.md); the unmodified arXiv
source is `paper/dimred-arxiv.tex`.  The figures and the bibliography file are not included.

## Repository layout

```
DimRed/
  MainTheorems.lean   the proved results, stated in full; the main theorems named
  Frozen/             the frozen statement surface, one frozen statement per file
    Main/             Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.1 (open)
    Prelim/           Section 2 (proved)
    BaseCase/         Section 3 (proved)
    Regularity/       the five clauses of Proposition 4.1 (open)
  Support/            the vocabulary and the lemmas the frozen statements are proved from
  Meta/               AxiomsAudit.lean
DimRed.lean           the root module (imports the whole library)
Audit/                README.md: the comparator challenges, to be added
ASSUMPTIONS.md        the cited results assumed (none; generated)
CORRESPONDENCE.md     paper ↔ Lean: vocabulary, standing hypotheses, node table
ledger/manifest.yaml  one row per registered statement: hash, paper source, state
paper/dimred.tex      the paper, pinned by the SHA-256 in ledger/manifest.yaml
paper/dimred-arxiv.tex  the unmodified arXiv source (v2)
paper/CHANGES_FROM_ARXIV.md  every difference between the two
tools/                the checkers and generators listed under Building
```

## How this was built

The Lean code in this repository was written by AI models under the supervision of the
author, in a private working repository whose history is this one.  A supervising Claude
Code session launched the agents below, wrote briefs, recorded the author's decisions and
merged their branches; for this repository it ran on Claude Fable 5.1 from 2026-09-10 to
2026-09-16, on Claude Opus 5 on 2026-09-19 and 2026-09-20, and on Claude Opus 5.5 on
2026-09-23 and 2026-09-24.  On
2026-09-10 a Claude Opus 5 general, a Claude Code subagent, froze all twenty statements,
wrote the vocabulary and sealed six of the Section 2 and 3 results; GLM-5.3-flash drafted
compile-filtered proof candidates for it, and GLM-5.3 and GLM-5.3-flash audited the frozen
statements against the paper, one audit forcing a transcription repair.  From 2026-09-13 to
2026-09-16 two DeepSeek-v4.1-flash generals, through a custom driver, sealed the derivative
comparison (Lemma 2.4), corrected the statements of Lemma 2.4 and Proposition 3.2, and proved
the block lemmas of Proposition 3.2; a Claude Sonnet 5 general completed Proposition 3.2 on
2026-09-16.  On 2026-09-19 the supervising session, then on Claude Opus 5, corrected the
statements of Corollary 2.1 and of the topple time and proved the two corollaries of Section 2,
and OpenAI's gpt-5.6-luna, through Codex at reasoning effort xhigh, wrote the support
modules for the regularity induction of Sections 4 and 5.  On 2026-09-20 Mistral's
Leanstral, through its `vibe` agent, filled the proofs of four support lemmas whose
statements the supervising session wrote: the existence of the topple time, weak facet
compatibility off the facet, the unit-gap branch of dimensional reduction and the weak facet
step of the induction; the supervising session committed them on 2026-09-20 and 2026-09-23.  Other attempts on the open nodes, by Claude Sonnet 5, DeepSeek and
Leanstral agents, are not in this repository.  Claude Opus 5.5 wrote
`DimRed/MainTheorems.lean`, the axiom audit and this documentation on 2026-09-24.  The
models, tooling, cost, and review status are disclosed in full in
[`formalization.yaml`](formalization.yaml), following the
[mathlib-initiative](https://github.com/mathlib-initiative/formalization.yaml) standard.
